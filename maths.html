Two products, two different jobs

The dot product returns a number and measures how much two vectors point the same way; it is zero exactly when they are perpendicular. The cross product returns a vector perpendicular to both, whose length is the area of the parallelogram they span; it is zero exactly when they are parallel. Almost every question is asking for one of those two facts.

Worked through

Example

Find the angle between a = î + ĵ and b = ĵ + k̂.

Answer: 60°.

a·b = 1, |a| = |b| = √2, so cosθ = 1/2.

The scalar triple product is a volume

[a b c] is the volume of the parallelepiped the three vectors span, with a sign for orientation. So [a b c] = 0 says the box has collapsed flat — the three vectors are coplanar. It is unchanged by a cyclic shift and changes sign under a swap, and any repeated vector kills it.

Matrix multiplication does not commute

AB and BA are usually different, and may not both exist. Nor does AB = 0 mean that A or B is zero. Transposes and inverses reverse the order: (AB)ᵀ = BᵀAᵀ and (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹.

Determinant properties do the work

Two equal rows make it zero; swapping two rows flips the sign; a common factor comes out of one row or column only. A triangular determinant is just the product of the diagonal. Most determinant questions are solved by row operations, not by expansion.

Worked through

Example

Evaluate |1 1 1; a b c; a² b² c²|.

Answer: (a − b)(b − c)(c − a).

It is the Vandermonde determinant.

One definition, three curves

Every conic is the set of points whose distance from a fixed focus is e times the distance from a fixed directrix. e = 1 gives a parabola, e < 1 an ellipse, e > 1 a hyperbola. Everything else — foci, latus rectum, asymptotes — follows from that one statement.

Worked through

Example

An ellipse has a = 5 and b = 3. Find its eccentricity and foci.

Answer: e = 0.8, foci at (±4, 0).

e² = 1 − b²/a² = 1 − 9/25, so e = 4/5; ae = 4.

Tangents

At a point on the curve the tangent is got by 'splitting' the equation: x² → xx₁, y² → yy₁, and so on — the rule written as T = 0. In slope form the condition for y = mx + c to touch is c² = a²m² + b² for the ellipse and c² = a²m² − b² for the hyperbola; the sign follows the conic's own.

Worked through

Answer: c = ±√40.

Choosing the first function: ILATE

For integration by parts take u in the order Inverse, Logarithmic, Algebraic, Trigonometric, Exponential. The point is to pick the part that becomes simpler when differentiated.

Worked through

Example

Answer: xeˣ − eˣ + C.

Algebraic before exponential: u = x, dv = eˣdx.

Two patterns worth recognising on sight

∫eˣ(f(x) + f′(x))dx = eˣf(x) + C, and ∫(xf′(x) + f(x))dx = xf(x) + C. Each turns what looks like a long integration by parts into a single line, and they are set every year.

Worked through

Example

Evaluate ∫eˣ(sin x + cos x)dx.

Answer: eˣ sin x + C.

f = sin x and f′ = cos x.

Substitutions that always work

For a rational function of sin and cos, t = tan(x/2). For √(a² − x²) put x = a sinθ; for √(a² + x²) put x = a tanθ; for √(x² − a²) put x = a secθ. For ax² + bx + c, complete the square and match a standard form.

Worked through

Example

Evaluate ∫dx/(x² + 4x + 13).

Answer: (1/3)tan⁻¹((x+2)/3) + C.

Complete the square: (x+2)² + 9.

FORMULA

Standard integrals

∫xⁿdx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1); ∫dx/x = ln|x|; ∫eᵗdx = eᵗ; ∫aᵗdx = aᵗ/ln a
The modulus in ln|x| matters — without it the answer is wrong for negative x. And the constant of integration is part of the answer.

Using it

Worked example

Evaluate ∫dx/x for x < 0.

Answer: ln|x| + C.

The modulus covers both signs.

Standard forms with a quadratic

∫dx/(x²+a²) = (1/a)tan⁻¹(x/a); ∫dx/√(a²−x²) = sin⁻¹(x/a)

FORMULA

Partial fractions and the tan(x/2) substitution

split by linear, repeated and quadratic factors; for sin and cos rationally, put t = tan(x/2)
Divide first if the numerator's degree is at least the denominator's. The half-angle substitution turns any rational function of sin and cos into a rational function of t.

Using it

Worked example

What substitution handles ∫dx/(3 + 2sin x)?

Answer: t = tan(x/2).

A rational function of sin x.
